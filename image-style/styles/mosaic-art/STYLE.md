# Mosaic Art

A photo rebuilt as a handmade mosaic of small glass and stone tiles set in grout, with tile rows that flow along the shapes and a slightly uneven, glinting surface.

## Prompt

```text
Convert the attached photo into an original handmade mosaic artwork made of real tiles, viewed straight on.

Tiles: build the entire image only from small, individually cut tesserae of colored glass, smalti, ceramic, and stone. Most tiles are roughly square, about 80 to 120 tiles across the width of the image, with some cut into wedges, triangles, and irregular pieces to fit curves and edges. Each tile has one main color, with slight variation inside it.

Grout: separate every tile with thin, visible grout lines in light gray or warm beige, so the whole image reads clearly as set tiles.

Flow: lay the tiles in rows that follow the contours of each shape, like ripples around the subject. Outline the main shapes with one or two rows of tiles in a darker or contrasting color, then fill their insides with rows that curve along the forms. Fill the background with calmer, straighter rows.

Subject: keep the photo's subject, setting, composition, and main colors recognizable. Build the subject itself entirely from tiles exactly like the background, so it never looks like a painting placed on top. There are absolutely no drawn, painted, or printed lines anywhere: every line, edge, and detail is made of tiles. Simplify thin parts boldly: render each thin part as a single row of tiles at least one tile wide, reduce many fine parts, such as spokes or wires, to a few wide tile rows, and leave out the finest parts, such as cables.

Faces: build faces and hands only from tiles. Eyes, eyebrows, noses, and mouths are a few small dark tiles, and skin shading comes only from changing tile colors. No painted facial features or smooth skin.

Color: rich, slightly varied tile colors based on the photo, with gentle shading made by changing tile colors from row to row, never by painted gradients across tiles.

Surface: real tiles with a slightly uneven, hand-set surface, small gaps, glossy glass tiles catching tiny highlights, and matte stone tiles. No photographic detail, paint, or flat digital squares.

Show only the mosaic filling the frame, with no frame, wall, border, or hands.

Don't include any text, letters, or watermarks.

Keep the same aspect ratio as the attached image.
```

## Observed in references

These traits come from viewing Google image results for "mosaic art" from the user's link and from the source below. The images weren't stored. Add the ones you want to the image prompt.

- **Subjects:** birds, animals, flowers, landscapes, and Mediterranean village streets dominate, in vivid colors.
- **Fine mosaics:** detailed works use tiny tiles in curving rows that follow fur, feathers, and faces, with dark outline rows around shapes.
- **Byzantine:** religious mosaics use gold glass tiles for halos and backgrounds.
- **Broken tile:** some craft works use large, irregular broken ceramic pieces with wide grout, closer to a hobby look.
- **Context:** many results show tiles, tools, hands, framed panels, or paper mosaics for kids, which this style leaves out.
- **Overlap:** `stained-glass` uses backlit glass joined by dark lead lines, `low-poly` uses seamless flat triangles, and `gustav-klimt` uses gold-leaf ornament, while this style uses small set tiles with grout lines.

## Examples

Without them, an earlier attempt drew the spokes and cables as thin lines and gave the faces painted-looking detail. This result has no text or frame, keeps the 3:2 ratio and the source's people, poses, smiles, clothing colors, bicycle, and street, and builds everything, including the faces, hands, and wide tile spokes, from small stone and glass tiles with grout lines and rows that follow the forms.
