# Paul Cézanne

A photo repainted as a Paul Cézanne-style Post-Impressionist oil painting, built from patches of parallel brushstrokes, solid geometric forms modeled with warm and cool color, and broken blue outlines.

## Prompt

```text
Convert the attached photo into an original oil painting in the style of Paul Cézanne's mature work (Post-Impressionism, 1880–1906). Don't copy any specific existing Cézanne painting.

Brushwork: build the whole picture from small, distinct patches of short, parallel brushstrokes, often slanting diagonally, laid side by side. Vary the patches in size, direction, and shape, mixing larger flat planes with smaller stroke groups, so they never look like a uniform grid of tiles or a mosaic. Each patch is a slightly different shade, so surfaces look constructed from many facets of color rather than smoothly blended.

Form: simplify everything into solid geometric volumes, such as cylinders, spheres, cones, and blocky planes, so people, objects, trees, and buildings look sturdy and weighty. Keep shapes slightly irregular and hand-made.

Modeling: shape volume with color temperature instead of dark shadows. Turn forms from warm colors in the light, such as ochre, orange, and yellow, to cool colors in the shade, such as blue, blue-green, and violet. Avoid black shadows.

Outline: draw clearly visible contours with broken, repeated lines of dark Prussian blue around people, main objects, and major shapes, left open in places so color patches pass through them.

Space: tilt surfaces such as tables, floors, and roads slightly up toward the viewer, and let small shifts in viewpoint appear between objects, while keeping the scene stable and balanced.

Color: a deep, earthy, saturated palette based on the photo's colors, with rich ochre, burnt orange, terracotta, olive and deep viridian green, strong Prussian blue, cerulean, warm grays, and touches of red, dominated by the balance of warm oranges and cool blues and greens. Avoid light, washed-out pastel tones.

Figures: paint people as heavy, solid, still, monumental volumes, like weighty sculpted forms, with simplified, slightly mask-like faces and few details, while keeping the photo's poses and expressions.

Subject: keep the photo's subject, setting, composition, clothing colors, and objects recognizable. Don't add people or objects that aren't in the photo.

Surface: oil paint on canvas with a matte, dry finish, visible brushstrokes, and a few small areas of thinly covered or bare canvas. No photographic detail, smooth blending, or digital gradients.

Show only the painting filling the frame, with no frame, wall, or border.

Don't include any text, signature, or watermark.

Keep the same aspect ratio as the attached image.
```

## Observed in references

These traits come from viewing Google image results for "paul cezanne paintings" and from the sources below. The images weren't stored. Add the ones you want to the image prompt.

- **Still lifes:** many works show apples, oranges, jugs, and crumpled white tablecloths on tilted tables.
- **Landscapes:** Provence views of a blue mountain, pines, rocks, and ochre houses with red roofs are common.
- **Portraits:** sitters such as card players and seated women are stiff, still, and solid, in red, blue, or brown clothing.
- **Watercolors:** late watercolors leave much of the paper bare between scattered patches of color, which this style doesn't follow.
- **Overlap:** `claude-monet` uses loose, broken strokes for changing light, and `cubist-illustration` uses flat overlapping planes with bold outlines, while this style keeps solid volumes built from parallel brushstroke patches.

The faces still keep fairly lively detail.
