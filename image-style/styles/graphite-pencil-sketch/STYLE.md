# Graphite Pencil Sketch

A photo redrawn as a hand-drawn graphite pencil sketch on paper, with loose searching lines, layered hatching, and a detailed focal area that fades into unfinished edges.

## Prompt

```text
Convert the attached photo into an original hand-drawn graphite pencil sketch on white or off-white drawing paper.

Line: draw with real pencil marks. Start from light, loose construction lines and searching contour lines, some repeated or overlapping, then firm up the main contours with darker, more confident strokes of varied pressure.

Tone: build values only with graphite, using directional hatching and crosshatching that follows the forms, plus soft smudged blending in the shadows. Range from the white of the paper to deep, rich graphite darks, with a slight metallic sheen in the darkest areas. Leave highlights as bare paper.

Focus: render the subject's focal area, such as faces, eyes, and hands, or the main object, with the most detail and contrast. Let the drawing become lighter, looser, and less finished toward the edges, with some areas left as quick outlines or blank paper.

Subject: keep the photo's subject, setting, composition, proportions, and expressions accurate and recognizable, drawn realistically by a skilled artist. Don't add objects that aren't in the photo.

Surface: visible paper tooth, graphite grain, and a few faint smudges or eraser marks. No color, ink lines, digital gradients, or photographic texture.

Show only the drawing filling the frame, with no pencils, hands, sketchbook edges, desk, or tape.

Don't include any text, signature, or watermark.

Keep the same aspect ratio as the attached image.
```

## Observed in references

These traits come from viewing Google image results for "pencil sketch style". The images weren't stored. Add the ones you want to the image prompt.

- **Portraits:** most results are portraits with highly detailed eyes and lips and loose, flowing hair strokes.
- **Expressive sketches:** some use energetic, scribbled line webs with visible construction lines.
- **Toned paper:** a few are drawn on warm beige paper with soft shading.
- **Context:** many results show the drawing with pencils, hands, or a sketchbook, which this style leaves out.
- **Overlap:** `colored-pencil-sketch` uses color and loose travel-sketch strokes, and `etching-engraving` uses fine ink lines, while this style uses graphite only.
