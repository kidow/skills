# Joseon Genre Painting

A photo redrawn as a late Joseon Korean genre painting, with lively ink brush lines and pale washes of color on aged paper.

## Prompt

```text
Convert the attached photo into an original painting in the style of late Joseon dynasty Korean genre painting (pungsokhwa, 18th century). Avoid copying any specific existing artwork.

Medium: ink and light color on aged paper or silk, like a page from an album. It must look hand-painted with a brush, with no digital rendering, photographic texture, or Western oil painting.

Line: the brush line leads. Draw figures and objects with confident dark ink outlines that vary from thick to thin, with a casual, quick, lively feel. Use sharp, angular strokes for clothing folds.

Color: pale, diluted washes of a few natural colors, such as muted gray-blue, soft brown, and ochre, over a light ink base. Colors stay transparent and restrained. Let the warm paper show through. No Western shading, cast shadows, or strong highlights.

Figures: keep the people, actions, and relationships from the photo as the main subject, and keep their clothing and objects. Simplify their faces with small, lively ink features and warm, candid expressions and gestures, with a gentle sense of humor. Draw people slightly small within the page, with natural, slightly naive proportions.

Background: omit most of the background and leave wide areas of empty paper. Suggest the setting with only a few loose ink strokes, such as a ground line, a patch of dry grass, or part of a wall, and only what's in the photo.

Composition: an uncluttered arrangement built on the figures' relationships, using a loose circle, diagonal, or X shape, with a flat, shallow sense of space.

Surface: warm beige paper with slight aging and soft stains.

Don't add text, calligraphy, signatures, or red seals.

Keep the same aspect ratio as the attached image.
```

## Observed in references

These traits come from viewing Google image results for Joseon genre paintings. The images weren't stored. Add the ones you want to the image prompt. This style follows the sparse Kim Hongdo approach by default.

- **Two approaches:** Kim Hongdo's works have pale colors and bare backgrounds, while Shin Yun-bok's have finer lines, brighter reds and blues, and detailed landscapes or architecture.
- **Trees and rocks:** when present, pines and rocks use dark, energetic ink with dry-brush texture.
- **Clothing:** white or pale robes dominate, with black hats as strong dark accents.
- **Page:** many works have a thin ruled border, a darker aged edge, a red seal, and calligraphy, which this style leaves out.
- **Paper tone:** tones range from pale beige to deep golden brown silk.

Without those additions, an earlier attempt had large figures, detailed modern faces, and a strong orange sweater. This result has no text or seal, keeps the 3:2 ratio and the source's clothing, and shows small figures, wide empty paper, pale washes, and only a wall, vine, and grass from the setting. Its paper is still golden rather than pale beige, its lines are thinner and cleaner than the references' energetic brushwork, and its faces still look like modern illustration.
